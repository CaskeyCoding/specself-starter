# When I've polished past the point of learning, I ship the rough version and let real players teach me, even when it's embarrassing.

State it as a rule I can follow and fail. The trigger is specific: *past the point
of learning* — when another day of solo polish tells me nothing a player wouldn't.

## Why

My worst months were spent perfecting games nobody played. Shipping rough is how I
convert opinion into evidence. The cost is looking unfinished in public; the
protection is that I stop wasting weeks on guesses.

## Evidence

Concrete, dated instances of me actually living (or failing) this.

- 2026-02-15 — Shipped a 48-hour game-jam build with placeholder art rather than
  spending the weekend on a menu nobody asked for. Two strangers' feedback
  reshaped the core loop in a day. See
  [`reflections/evidence/2026-02-15-jam-game-shipped.md`](../../reflections/evidence/2026-02-15-jam-game-shipped.md).
- 2025-11-03 — *Failed* this: sat on a demo for five weeks "until the lighting was
  right." Wishlists were flat the whole time. The lighting changed nothing.

## Falsification

If shipping rough repeatedly produced noise instead of signal — players bouncing on
bugs before they reach the idea — this moves to `contested`. One embarrassing launch
that teaches nothing is not falsification; a pattern of them is.

## Tensions

Collides with a quiet pride in craft. Under deadline pressure I resolve it toward
shipping; the principle exists precisely because my default is the other way.
